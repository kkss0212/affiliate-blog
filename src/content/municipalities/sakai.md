Sakai's most striking feature predates the city's historical records
entirely: Daisen Kofun, a massive keyhole-shaped burial mound traditionally
identified as the tomb of Emperor Nintoku, is among the largest tombs
anywhere in the world by ground area — larger, by that measure, than Egypt's
Great Pyramid of Giza or China's First Emperor's mausoleum complex, though
its low, forested profile makes it far less visually dramatic at ground
level. It anchors the Mozu-Furuichi Kofun Group, a cluster of similar 4th-
and 5th-century burial mounds across Sakai and neighboring cities that
became a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2019.

### A merchant republic, briefly

Sakai's second defining era came over a millennium later, during the
politically fragmented Sengoku period, when the city's wealthy merchant
class secured effective self-governance and grew rich on trade — including,
notably, importing and manufacturing firearms after the Portuguese
introduced them to Japan in 1543. Contemporary European visitors, including
Jesuit missionaries, reportedly described Sakai's self-governing merchant
structure as comparable to the Italian city-states, Venice in particular —
an unusual arrangement in a country otherwise organized around feudal
domains, and one that ended when Oda Nobunaga and later the unified
Tokugawa state brought Sakai firmly under central authority.

### From gunsmiths to chef's knives

Sakai's Sengoku-era gunsmithing expertise didn't disappear once firearms
production declined — it redirected into bladesmithing more broadly, and
the city's swordsmiths and blacksmiths gradually built a reputation for
blade quality that, by the modern era, had shifted mostly toward kitchen
knives. Today Sakai supplies roughly 90% of Japan's professional-grade
kitchen knife market, and its knives are widely sought after by chefs
internationally as some of the finest available.

## Getting there and getting around

Sakai sits directly south of Osaka City and is reachable from central Osaka
in about 20–30 minutes by train. Daisen Kofun and the surrounding kofun
cluster are within walking distance of Mozu Station; the city's traditional
crafts district is a separate area better reached by a short additional
train or bus ride.
