Attack on Titan opens with a premise simple enough to fit in a sentence —
humanity lives walled in, hunted by giant humanoid Titans — and spends its
entire 139-chapter run methodically complicating that premise until almost
nothing about the initial setup means what it first appeared to. Hajime
Isayama, born in Oyama (now part of Hita City), Oita Prefecture, serialized
the series in Bessatsu Shonen Magazine from 2009 to 2021, and its shift
from straightforward monster-horror into a genuinely dense political epic
is a big part of why it's stayed a fixture of "serious" manga discussion
well past its initial action-horror hook.

### Anime's answer to Game of Thrones, by critical consensus

That political complexity is exactly why Attack on Titan gets compared to
Game of Thrones so consistently in Western coverage — multiple outlets,
independently of each other, have used almost that exact phrase. Both
stories kill off major characters without narrative warning, build entire
factions around genuinely defensible but incompatible worldviews, and
resist the clean good-versus-evil framing a lot of action fantasy defaults
to. It's a comparison that holds up on a structural level, not just a "both
have dark tone" level, which is part of why it's followed the series into
so much of its English-language critical reception.

### From niche horror manga to chart-topping phenomenon

Attack on Titan's commercial trajectory outpaced even its own genre's
expectations — for the first half of 2014, it topped Oricon's comics
chart, briefly outselling even One Piece during that stretch, an
unusual feat for a series built on Titans eating people rather than a more
conventional Shonen adventure hook. Industry recognition followed
alongside the commercial success: the 2011 Kodansha Manga Award, and a
nomination the following year for the Osamu Tezuka Cultural Prize, one of
manga's most prestigious honors.

## Where to start

Volume 1 is worth starting with even if you've only seen the anime — the
manga's pacing and Isayama's early, rougher art style give a different
sense of the story's horror-first roots before it expands into the wider
political epic. If you'd rather watch, the original 2013 anime season
(Wit Studio) covers the same opening material before the series later
moved to MAPPA for its final seasons.
