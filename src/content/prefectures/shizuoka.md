Shizuoka runs along a long stretch of Pacific coastline directly on the
old Tokaido — the road connecting Edo (Tokyo) to Kyoto during the Edo
period — which made it one of the most-depicted and most-traveled parts
of the country for centuries before the railway existed.

### Shizuoka and the wider world

That road produced one of Japan's most globally influential art series:
Utagawa Hiroshige's "The Fifty-Three Stations of the Tokaido," a set of
woodblock prints depicting stops along the route, includes numerous
stations located in Shizuoka. Ukiyo-e prints like Hiroshige's and
Hokusai's — flat perspective, bold outlines, unusual cropping — reached
Europe in large numbers in the mid-to-late 1800s and became a documented,
significant influence on Impressionist and Post-Impressionist painters,
including Van Gogh and Monet, a movement art historians now call
Japonisme. Shizuoka's stretch of the Tokaido, and its views of Mount
Fuji from the coast, were part of the raw material for that whole
tradition.

Shizuoka's other major historical figure needs no introduction within
Japan: Tokugawa Ieyasu, founder of the roughly 250-year Edo shogunate,
retired to Sunpu Castle here after handing formal power to his son, and
died in the city in 1616. He was first enshrined at nearby Kunozan
Toshogu before a second shrine was built in his honor the following year
at Nikko, in Tochigi — making Shizuoka, not Tochigi, the site of his
original mausoleum.

### Neighbors: Shizuoka, Yamanashi, and Aichi

Shizuoka shares Mount Fuji, and a long-running good-natured rivalry over
it, with Yamanashi to the north. Internally, the prefecture also splits
somewhat between its Shizuoka City-centered east and Hamamatsu-centered
west, with Hamamatsu's economy and culture leaning noticeably toward
neighboring Aichi and the Nagoya region.

## Getting there and getting around

Shizuoka Station and Hamamatsu Station are both Tokaido Shinkansen stops,
roughly 1-1.5 hours from Tokyo. Kunozan Toshogu and Nihondaira are
reached from Shizuoka City by bus, taxi, or the Nihondaira Ropeway.
