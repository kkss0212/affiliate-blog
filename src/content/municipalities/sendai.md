Sendai's founding is inseparable from one man: Date Masamune, the one-eyed
daimyo (nicknamed "the One-Eyed Dragon") who built his castle here in 1600
after backing the winning side at the Battle of Sekigahara. Masamune
deliberately planted the city with trees as part of its original design — a
practical windbreak measure that also became an aesthetic signature,
producing the "Mori no Miyako" (City of Trees) identity Sendai still
promotes today, most visibly along the broad, leafy Jozenji-dori avenue
through the modern city center.

### An early, ambitious embassy to Rome

Masamune's most striking brush with the wider world came in 1613, when he
sponsored the Keicho Embassy — a mission led by samurai Hasekura Tsunenaga
that sailed from the Tohoku coast to Mexico, then on to Spain and Rome, where
Hasekura met King Philip III and Pope Paul V. It remains one of the earliest
and most ambitious diplomatic ventures any Japanese domain undertook toward
Europe, decades before Japan's long period of national isolation closed off
that kind of direct contact for over two centuries.

### Sendai, the Tohoku region, and 2011

As Tohoku's largest city and clear regional hub, Sendai anchors the six-
prefecture region both economically and administratively — Tohoku University,
founded in 1907, is one of Japan's oldest imperial universities and remains a
major research draw. Sendai's built-up center sits several kilometers inland
and was largely spared the worst structural damage of the March 2011 Tohoku
earthquake and tsunami, but the city's own coastal wards, like the rest of
the Tohoku coastline, were hit hard, and Sendai played (and continues to
play) a major logistical role in the region's ongoing recovery.

## Getting there and getting around

Sendai Station is a major Tohoku Shinkansen stop, roughly 1 hour 30 minutes
from Tokyo, and Sendai Airport connects to a handful of domestic and
international routes. The city has a compact subway network and an
extensive bus system; central Sendai is easily walkable, particularly along
Jozenji-dori and the nearby covered shopping arcades.
