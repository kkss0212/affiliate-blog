Kochi is remote and mountainous even by Shikoku standards, cut off from
the rest of the island by a mountain range — a geography that may help
explain why it produced one of the most independent-minded, and most
beloved, figures in modern Japanese history.

### Kochi and the wider world

Sakamoto Ryoma was a low-ranking samurai from Kochi who came to believe,
earlier and more completely than most of his contemporaries, that Japan
needed to adopt Western ships, weapons, and political ideas to avoid the
fate of other Asian countries being colonized by Western powers. He
purchased Western arms for allied domains, drafted an early proposal for
a parliamentary system of government, and — in an image still widely
reproduced today — was famously photographed wearing Western leather
boots with his traditional samurai clothing, a small but pointed signal
of where he thought Japan needed to go. His most consequential act was
diplomatic rather than technological: in 1866, he secretly brokered an
alliance between Satsuma (modern Kagoshima) and Choshu (modern
Yamaguchi), two rival domains whose combined military strength made
overthrowing the shogunate realistic for the first time. Ryoma was
assassinated in Kyoto in November 1867, on his 33rd birthday by the old
lunar calendar, just weeks before the shogunate formally surrendered
power — never seeing the country he'd helped remake. He remains one of
the most consistently popular figures in Japanese public opinion polls
about admired historical figures, over 150 years later.

### Neighbors: Kochi, Kagoshima, and Yamaguchi

Ryoma's Satsuma-Choshu alliance directly connects Kochi's history to two
other prefectures on this site — Yamaguchi (Choshu) and, further along
in Kyushu, Kagoshima (Satsuma) — three separate corners of Japan whose
local histories all converge on the same few years in the 1860s.

## Getting there and getting around

Kochi has no shinkansen connection; Kochi Ryoma Airport has domestic
flights, and Kochi Station is reached from Okayama by limited express
train in about 2.5 hours. A car is useful for reaching the Shimanto River
area in the prefecture's rural west.
