Kagawa is Japan's smallest prefecture by area, and has responded to that
fact not with any sense of diminishment but by concentrating its
identity into a couple of things it does more intensely than anywhere
else — udon noodles, and, more recently, art.

### Kagawa and the wider world

Starting in the 1990s, the Fukutake Foundation (now Benesse Holdings)
began a deliberate, long-term project to bring world-class contemporary
art to Naoshima and several neighboring islands in the Seto Inland
Sea — commissioning the architect Tadao Ando to design museum buildings
built partly underground to preserve the islands' landscapes, and
installing permanent works by international artists including James
Turrell and a dedicated gallery for a series of Claude Monet water lily
paintings. What began as a revitalization effort for small, depopulating
fishing islands has become one of the most internationally acclaimed
contemporary art destinations in the world, drawing visitors and
critics specifically for the art rather than any more conventional
tourist draw — a genuinely unusual trajectory for a former Seto Inland
Sea backwater.

### Neighbors: Kagawa and the rest of Shikoku

Kotohira-gu, one of Shikoku's most significant pilgrimage sites, draws
visitors from across the island independent of the more famous 88-temple
Shikoku Henro circuit that passes through neighboring Tokushima, Ehime,
and Kochi — Kagawa's own religious pull runs in parallel to, rather than
as part of, that shared island-wide pilgrimage tradition.

## Getting there and getting around

Takamatsu is reached from Okayama by direct limited express train in
about an hour, crossing the Seto Ohashi Bridge. Naoshima is a short ferry
ride from Takamatsu port or from Uno, on the Okayama side.
