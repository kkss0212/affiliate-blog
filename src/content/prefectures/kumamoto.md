Kumamoto sits at the geological and, at one pivotal moment, the
political center of Kyushu — home to one of the world's great volcanic
calderas, and to a castle whose 19th-century siege became a symbol of
old Japan's last serious military resistance to the new order replacing
it.

### Kumamoto and the wider world

In 1877, Kumamoto Castle came under siege by forces loyal to Saigo
Takamori, a former Meiji government leader who broke with the
modernizing state to lead the Satsuma Rebellion — the last major armed
uprising by Japan's samurai class, and the historical episode the film
"The Last Samurai" draws loosely on. The castle's garrison, defending on
behalf of the new Meiji government's Western-style conscript army,
held out for 53 days against Saigo's larger samurai force before
reinforcements arrived, a resistance still cited in Japan as proof of
Kumamoto Castle's near-legendary defensive design.

Kumamoto's more recent global export needed no siege at all. Kumamon, a
black bear mascot created in 2010 as part of a regional marketing push
tied to a new Kyushu Shinkansen line, became a genuine commercial
phenomenon — regularly topping national mascot popularity rankings,
generating enormous merchandise revenue, and eventually appearing in
international marketing partnerships and retail collaborations abroad,
making Kumamoto's fictional bear a more globally recognized ambassador
for the prefecture than almost any of its actual landmarks.

### Neighbors: Kumamoto and Kagoshima

The Satsuma Rebellion that besieged Kumamoto Castle was led from
Satsuma, in what's now Kagoshima to the south — the same rebellion that
ended with Saigo Takamori's death and is central to how Kagoshima tells
its own history.

## Getting there and getting around

Kumamoto Station is a Kyushu Shinkansen stop, about 45 minutes from
Hakata (Fukuoka). Mount Aso and Kurokawa Onsen are best reached by bus or
rental car from Kumamoto City.
