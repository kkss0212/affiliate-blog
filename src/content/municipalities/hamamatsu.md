Tokugawa Ieyasu ruled from Hamamatsu Castle for roughly 17 years in the
1570s–80s, a formative stretch during which he survived one of the closest
calls of his career — a crushing defeat at the nearby Battle of Mikatagahara
against Takeda Shingen — before eventually going on to unify Japan and found
the Edo shogunate. That trajectory gave the castle its enduring nickname,
Shusse-jo, the "Castle of Success," and later Tokugawa-era custom held that
serving as Hamamatsu's lord was a promising step toward higher office in
the shogunate.

### An unlikely capital of pianos and engines

Hamamatsu's modern global reputation rests on a coincidence of 19th- and
20th-century entrepreneurship rather than any obvious natural resource.
Torakusu Yamaha began repairing reed organs here in the 1880s, founding what
became Yamaha Corporation; a rival piano maker, Kawai, was founded here too
after splitting off from Yamaha in the 1920s, making Hamamatsu one of the
world's most concentrated centers of piano manufacturing. The same
mechanical and manufacturing know-how base later fed a motorcycle and engine
industry: Suzuki was founded in Hamamatsu (initially as a loom
manufacturer), and Honda's founder, Soichiro Honda, also got his start
repairing and racing vehicles in the city before eventually moving Honda's
main operations elsewhere.

### A large, spread-out city

The 2005 merger of Hamamatsu with a dozen surrounding municipalities made it
the largest of Japan's twenty designated cities by land area, stretching
from Lake Hamana and the Pacific coast inland toward mountainous terrain —
a footprint that gives the city genuinely varied geography, from dune
beaches to farmland to forested hills, unusual for a city of its
population.

## Getting there and getting around

Hamamatsu Station is a Tokaido Shinkansen stop, about 1 hour 40 minutes from
Tokyo and 40 minutes from Nagoya. The compact central area is walkable, but
Lake Hamana and the Nakatajima Dunes are more easily reached by car or bus
given the city's large, spread-out footprint.
