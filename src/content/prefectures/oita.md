Oita's identity is built on water rising out of the ground hot — it has
more individual hot spring sources, and produces more hot spring water
by total volume, than any other prefecture in Japan, and the whole
regional economy and culture organizes around that fact.

### Oita and the wider world

Decades before Nagasaki's Dejima formalized Japan's narrow, controlled
channel to the West, one of the country's very first documented direct
encounters with an Englishman happened by accident on Oita's coast. In
April 1600, the Dutch trading ship Liefde, its crew decimated by disease
and starvation after a disastrous Pacific crossing, ran aground off
Bungo Province — roughly modern Oita. Among the handful of survivors was
William Adams, an English navigator who was brought before Tokugawa
Ieyasu (soon to become the founder of the Edo shogunate), impressed him
with his knowledge of shipbuilding, mathematics, and navigation, and was
eventually granted samurai status, a Japanese name (Miura Anjin), and a
fief of his own — becoming a genuine bridge figure between Japan and the
West a full half-century before Dejima existed. His story became the
basis for James Clavell's 1975 novel "Shogun," later adapted into a hit
television series.

### Neighbors: Oita and the rest of Kyushu

Oita's accidental, early contact with the West predates and contrasts
with Nagasaki's later, tightly controlled arrangement at Dejima — one
prefecture's story begins with a shipwreck and an improvised alliance,
the other with two centuries of careful, bureaucratic management of
exactly the same kind of contact.

## Getting there and getting around

Oita Airport has domestic flights, and Oita Station is reached from
Hakata (Fukuoka) by limited express train in about 2 hours. Beppu and
Yufuin are both easily reached from Oita City by local or limited
express train.
