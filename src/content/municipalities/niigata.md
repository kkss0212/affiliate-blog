Niigata's inclusion among the ports opened under the 1858 treaties with the
Western powers makes it geographically unusual among Japan's early points of
foreign contact: while Yokohama, Kobe, and Nagasaki all face the Pacific
side, Niigata opened directly onto the Sea of Japan, historically Japan's
closest maritime frontier with Russia, Korea, and continental Northeast
Asia. In practice, Niigata's actual volume of foreign trade in the treaty-
port era lagged behind its Pacific-facing counterparts, partly due to its
harsh winter weather and a shifting river mouth that made the harbor
difficult to maintain — but the designation still shaped the city's early
modern development and self-image as a gateway city.

### Rice, snow, and sake

Niigata's flat delta land at the mouth of the Shinano River, Japan's
longest, combined with heavy winter snowfall that feeds abundant meltwater
in spring, makes the surrounding plain one of Japan's most productive rice-
growing regions — Niigata Prefecture, anchored by the city, is consistently
among Japan's top rice producers by volume, and the premium Koshihikari
variety grown in the Uonuma area carries a national reputation. That same
combination of good rice, cold winters, and clean snowmelt water has made
Niigata one of Japan's most respected sake-producing regions, with a dense
concentration of breweries both in the city and across the wider prefecture.

### A gateway that never quite became a Yokohama

Niigata never grew into a first-tier Japanese city the way its fellow treaty
ports Yokohama and Kobe did, in part because the Sea of Japan side of the
country industrialized more slowly than the Pacific corridor connecting
Tokyo, Nagoya, and Osaka. Today the city functions primarily as the
administrative and economic hub for Niigata Prefecture and the wider
Hokuriku/Sea of Japan coastal region, rather than as a major international
gateway in its own right.

## Getting there and getting around

Niigata Station is a Joetsu Shinkansen terminus, about 2 hours from Tokyo,
and Niigata Airport has some international connections, particularly within
Northeast Asia. The city center is flat and compact, well suited to cycling
and walking, with buses covering the rest of the metropolitan area.
