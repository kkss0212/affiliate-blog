Hokkaido is, administratively, not even called a prefecture — it's a "do"
(道), a distinct designation shared with no other part of Japan — and for
good reason: it joined the country later and differently than everywhere
else. For centuries it was Ezo, home to the indigenous Ainu people, only
brought under direct Japanese administration in 1869, the year after the
Meiji Restoration, as the new government pushed to settle and develop its
northern frontier before Russia could claim it first.

### Hokkaido and the wider world

That development effort leaned heavily on Western expertise. The Meiji
government hired foreign advisors — most famously the American agricultural
scientist William S. Clark, who spent less than a year in 1876 founding
Sapporo Agricultural College (now Hokkaido University) but left behind the
parting words "Boys, be ambitious," still one of the most quoted phrases in
Japanese education. Hokkaido's grid-planned streets, dairy industry, and
beer brewing (Sapporo Breweries was founded here in 1876) all trace back
to this deliberately Western-influenced settlement push.

The island's more difficult modern history is the position of the Ainu
themselves, whose land and language rights were suppressed for over a
century after annexation; Japan did not formally recognize the Ainu as an
indigenous people in law until 2019. Hokkaido's northernmost edge also
borders Russia's Kuril Islands, a territorial dispute — the "Northern
Territories" — that remains unresolved since the end of World War II.

### Hokkaido and the mainland

Hokkaido is connected to Honshu only by the Seikan Tunnel, one of the
world's longest undersea rail tunnels, linking Hakodate to Aomori
Prefecture in the Tohoku region — there's no land border with any other
prefecture, which shapes a lot of the island's distinct, semi-separate
identity within Japan.

## Getting there and getting around

New Chitose Airport, south of Sapporo, is Hokkaido's main air gateway,
with direct flights from several international hubs. The island is large
enough that a rental car is genuinely useful outside Sapporo itself, though
JR Hokkaido's rail network covers the main cities.
