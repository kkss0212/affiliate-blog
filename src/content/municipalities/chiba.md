Chiba's name comes directly from the Chiba clan, a powerful samurai family
who built a castle at Inohana, overlooking Tokyo Bay, in the 12th century
and dominated the surrounding Shimosa province for generations during the
Kamakura and Muromachi periods. Long before that, though, the area was
already a significant site of human settlement: the Kasori Shell Mounds,
just outside the modern city center, preserve one of the largest known
Jomon-period shell mound complexes anywhere in the world, built up over
roughly 2,000 years of coastal habitation and shellfish harvesting starting
around 5,000 years ago.

### A reclaimed-land convention capital

Chiba's most visible modern institution, Makuhari Messe, sits on land
reclaimed from Tokyo Bay starting in the 1980s as part of a broader push to
develop Chiba's waterfront for business and international events. Despite
the persistent branding confusion — the Tokyo Game Show, Anime Japan's
occasional Chiba runs, and several major international trade fairs are all
held here, not in Tokyo proper — Makuhari Messe has become one of Japan's
most important convention venues, drawing large annual crowds from across
the games, anime, and manga industries specifically because Tokyo itself
lacks comparable large-scale exhibition space.

### Chiba and greater Tokyo

Chiba City functions as the administrative capital of Chiba Prefecture and
a significant industrial port in its own right, part of the heavily
developed Keiyo industrial coastal belt that also includes Ichihara and
other Tokyo Bay cities. It sits at something of a remove from Chiba
Prefecture's most internationally famous address — Narita International
Airport and Tokyo Disney Resort are both elsewhere in the prefecture, not
in Chiba City itself, a distinction that occasionally trips up visitors
who assume "Chiba" always means one of those two destinations.

## Getting there and getting around

Chiba Station is about 40 minutes from central Tokyo by JR, and Makuhari
Messe has its own dedicated stations on the Keiyo Line. The city's main
sights are spread across several distinct areas — central Chiba, the
Kasori mounds, and the Makuhari waterfront — better connected by train or
bus than covered entirely on foot.
