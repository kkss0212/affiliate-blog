One Piece follows Monkey D. Luffy, a young man who gains the ability to
stretch his body like rubber after eating a mysterious "Devil Fruit," as he
assembles a crew and sails toward the title's namesake treasure in pursuit
of becoming King of the Pirates. It's a simple enough premise to explain in
a sentence, which is part of why it's stayed legible across more than a
thousand chapters and 25-plus years of continuous weekly serialization —
Eiichiro Oda has kept adding to the same core cast and world rather than
rebooting or replacing it.

### From Kumamoto to the best-selling manga in history

Oda, born in Kumamoto Prefecture in 1975, has cited Akira Toriyama's Dragon
Ball as a formative influence, and worked as an assistant to established
manga artists before Weekly Shonen Jump picked up One Piece in 1997. The
series has since sold more copies than any other manga in history — a
figure regularly updated but consistently cited in the hundreds of
millions — and holds a Guinness World Record for the most copies ever
published of a comic book series by a single author. Toei Animation's anime
adaptation, airing continuously since 1999, has become one of the longest-
running anime series in the medium's history.

### The live-action adaptation that actually worked

For years, live-action adaptations of beloved anime and manga had a
reliably poor reputation, both in Japan and internationally, dogged by
rushed productions and a mismatch between hand-drawn stylization and live
filming. Netflix's 2023 One Piece series broke that pattern unusually
cleanly, earning strong reviews from both longtime fans and new viewers and
becoming one of the platform's most-watched original series in its debut
window. Its success functioned as a significant on-ramp for viewers outside
Japan who'd never read the manga or watched the anime, feeding a fresh wave
of interest into the source material it was based on.

## Where to start

Because One Piece is so long-running, most new readers start from Volume 1
rather than jumping in mid-series — the early chapters move quickly and the
world-building rewards following it in order. Readers who want a faster way
in sometimes start with the Netflix live-action series as a compressed
introduction to the East Blue arc before moving to the manga or anime for
everything that follows.
