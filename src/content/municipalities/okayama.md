Okayama has built its entire modern civic identity around a single folk
tale: Momotaro, the story of a boy born from a giant peach who grows up to
defeat a band of marauding ogres (oni) with the help of a dog, a monkey, and
a pheasant. The legend has no single confirmed origin, but one of the most
widely repeated local theories ties it to the Kibitsu Shrine area just
outside the city, linking the story's ogre-defeating hero to a semi-
legendary local prince said to have subdued a bandit chief in ancient times.
Whatever its true origin, Okayama has leaned into the connection completely
— Momotaro statues, decorations, and branding are everywhere from the
station's main pedestrian avenue to local mascots and souvenirs.

### A garden built for a lord, not to hide from him

Korakuen, immediately across the river from Okayama Castle, was built in the
late 1600s as the domain lord's private garden and is unusual among
Japan's most celebrated gardens for its open lawns and long sightlines,
designed deliberately to be viewed and enjoyed from multiple vantage points
rather than experienced as a series of enclosed, secluded scenes — a
distinctive approach that helped it earn a place among Japan's "three great
gardens" alongside Kanazawa's Kenrokuen and Mito's Kairakuen.

### Okayama as a transport gateway

Since the 1988 opening of the Great Seto Bridge, one of the world's longest
two-tier bridge systems, Okayama has functioned as the primary rail and
road gateway between Honshu and Shikoku, a role reinforced by its position
as a major Sanyo Shinkansen junction. That gateway status makes Okayama a
common transfer point for travelers heading onward to Shikoku rather than
always a final destination in its own right.

## Getting there and getting around

Okayama Station is a Sanyo Shinkansen stop, about 3 hours 20 minutes from
Tokyo and 45 minutes from Osaka, and the departure point for Shinkansen and
expressway connections into Shikoku. Central Okayama, including Korakuen
and the castle, is easily walkable or reachable by streetcar from the
station.
