Okinawa was not always Japanese. For roughly 450 years, from 1429 until
1879, it was the Ryukyu Kingdom — an independent maritime trading state
whose ships connected China, Japan, Korea, and Southeast Asia, and whose
court culture, language, and religion developed on a separate track from
the rest of Japan. Even today, the Ryukyuan languages are considered
linguistically distinct enough from mainland Japanese that some
linguists classify them as a separate branch of the Japonic language
family, not a dialect.

### Okinawa and the wider world

Okinawa's relationship with outside powers has been unusually intense for
its size. The Ryukyu Kingdom itself was a tributary state of Ming and Qing
China for centuries, absorbing Chinese architectural and administrative
influence, before Japan formally annexed it as a prefecture in 1879 (a
process known as the Ryukyu Disposition). Then, in April-June 1945, Okinawa
became the site of the Battle of Okinawa — the largest amphibious assault
of the Pacific War and one of its bloodiest campaigns, with estimated
civilian deaths in the tens of thousands, a substantial share of the
prewar population.

The US administered Okinawa directly for 27 years afterward, not
returning it to Japanese sovereignty until 1972 — a full two decades after
the rest of Japan regained independence in 1952. US military bases still
occupy a significant share of Okinawa's main island today, a presence that
remains a genuinely debated political issue locally, distinct from how
the rest of Japan experiences the US-Japan security relationship.

### Okinawa and mainland Japan

Precisely because of this history, Okinawa's relationship with "mainland"
Japan (naichi, in local usage) carries more cultural distance than the
usual prefecture-to-prefecture rivalry seen elsewhere in this guide —
closer to how Hawaii relates to the continental United States than how,
say, Osaka relates to Tokyo.

## Getting there and getting around

Naha Airport is Okinawa's main air gateway, with direct international
flights from several East Asian hubs. Okinawa is an island chain, not a
single landmass — getting to the outer islands (Miyako, Ishigaki, Kerama)
generally means a short domestic flight or ferry from Naha.
