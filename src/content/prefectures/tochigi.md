Tochigi's identity runs through two very different small towns: Nikko, a
shrine town built around the mausoleum of the man who founded the Edo
shogunate, and Mashiko, a pottery town whose quiet 20th-century
collaborations helped reshape ceramics on the other side of the world.

### Tochigi and the wider world

In the early 1920s, the English potter Bernard Leach moved to Japan and
worked closely with Shoji Hamada, a young Japanese potter from Mashiko,
before the two traveled together to set up Leach's pottery studio in
Cornwall, England. That collaboration — and Hamada's subsequent decades
of work in Mashiko, where he became a government-designated Living
National Treasure — is widely credited with introducing Japanese folk
pottery aesthetics (mingei) to Western studio ceramics, an influence
still visible in "wood-fired," "wabi-sabi"-inflected pottery made across
Europe and North America today.

Nikko had its own, earlier brush with Western attention: the British
travel writer Isabella Bird visited in 1878 during her journey through
rural Japan, and her book "Unbeaten Tracks in Japan" gave English-language
readers one of their first detailed accounts of Nikko Toshogu's carved
buildings — including, most famously, the "see no evil, hear no evil,
speak no evil" three monkeys carved into the shrine's stables, an image
that would go on to become one of the most reproduced pieces of Japanese
iconography anywhere in the world.

### Neighbors: Tochigi and the rest of Kita-Kanto

Tochigi, Ibaraki, and Gunma are often grouped together informally as
"Kita-Kanto" (North Kanto) — Tokyo's less-famous northern neighbors,
sharing a similar rural-commuter-belt identity and, often, similarly low
rankings in Japan's national prefectural "attractiveness" surveys, which
locals across all three tend to shrug off with matching irony.

## Getting there and getting around

Utsunomiya is a Tohoku Shinkansen stop, about 50 minutes from Tokyo;
Nikko is reached from Utsunomiya by a further local or limited express
train, or directly from Tokyo via the Tobu Nikko Line.
