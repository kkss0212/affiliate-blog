No prefecture on this site has a more literal connection to Japan's
relationship with the West than Kanagawa — it's not a connection this
page has to go looking for, because the connection is in the name.

### Kanagawa and the wider world

In 1854, after Commodore Matthew Perry's US Navy squadron returned with a
show of force following his first visit the previous year, Japanese and
American negotiators signed the Convention of Kanagawa here, ending over
two centuries of the Tokugawa shogunate's sakoku (closed-country) policy
and opening two ports to American ships. A follow-up treaty in 1858 then
designated Yokohama, a small fishing village at the time, as one of
Japan's first treaty ports open to full foreign trade and residence —
and Yokohama grew explosively from that moment on, developing Japan's
largest Chinatown and a lasting identity as the country's most
internationally minded major city, a role Kanagawa arguably still plays
today as home to more resident foreign nationals than almost any other
prefecture.

Kanagawa's older brush with reshaping Japan came from within: in 1185,
the samurai leader Minamoto no Yoritomo established his government in
Kamakura rather than the imperial capital of Kyoto, founding Japan's
first shogunate and beginning nearly 700 years of military, rather than
imperial court, rule — a shift in the country's center of gravity not
unlike the one Tokyo would pull off centuries later at Kyoto's expense
again.

### Neighbors: Kanagawa and Tokyo

Kanagawa borders Tokyo directly to the south and functions as its most
economically integrated neighbor — Yokohama is Japan's second-largest
city by population, but has never seriously competed with Tokyo for
national dominance, settling instead into a permanent, comfortable role
as the country's biggest and most historically international "second
city."

## Getting there and getting around

Yokohama and Kamakura are both easily reached from central Tokyo by
regular train in 30-60 minutes; Hakone is about 90 minutes via the Odakyu
Romancecar limited express. Shin-Yokohama is also a Tokaido Shinkansen
stop for travelers heading further west.
