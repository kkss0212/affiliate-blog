Aomori sits at the northern tip of Honshu, close enough to Hokkaido that the
two are linked by the undersea Seikan Tunnel — for most of Japanese
history this made Aomori a genuine frontier, the last stop before the
country's most recently settled territory.

### Aomori and the wider world

Aomori's clearest link to deep history predates any contact with the West
by thousands of years: the Sannai-Maruyama archaeological site, excavated
starting in 1992, revealed one of the largest and longest-inhabited
settlements of the Jomon period — Japan's Neolithic hunter-gatherer
culture, active from roughly 14,000 to 300 BC. It's now part of a
UNESCO World Heritage listing covering Jomon sites across northern Japan,
giving Aomori an unusually deep claim on "oldest," predating the
Yayoi and Kofun-period states that most of Japan's history is built on.

Its modern international connection runs through the sea rather than any
single treaty port: Aomori's ports and fisheries (particularly its
scallop and tuna industries) have long exported to markets across East
Asia and beyond, and the completion of the Seikan Tunnel in 1988 —
at the time the world's longest and deepest undersea tunnel — was itself
an engineering feat closely watched internationally.

### Neighbors: Aomori and the rest of Tohoku

Aomori anchors the northern end of the Tohoku region, historically
considered peripheral to the Kansai/Kanto power centers further south — a
shared identity across Tohoku's six prefectures as Japan's rural,
agricultural north, though each retains a distinct dialect and local
festival culture (Aomori's Nebuta, Akita's Kanto, and Sendai's Tanabata
are collectively promoted as the "three great festivals of Tohoku").

## Getting there and getting around

Aomori Airport and Shin-Aomori Station (the Tohoku Shinkansen's
northern terminus) are the main gateways; from Shin-Aomori, the Hokkaido
Shinkansen continues on through the Seikan Tunnel to Hakodate. A rental
car is useful for reaching Oirase Gorge and Lake Towada, which aren't
well served by rail.
