X Japan started with two kindergarten friends in Tateyama, a small city on
the Boso Peninsula in Chiba Prefecture: Yoshiki, on drums and piano, and
Toshi, on vocals, who formed the band together in 1982 under a series of
earlier names before settling on "X." Both had been on track for more
conventional paths — Yoshiki toward classical music training, Toshi toward
medical school — before relocating to Tokyo in 1984 to chase music
professionally instead, a decision that ended up shaping the sound of
Japanese rock for decades afterward.

### Inventing a genre almost by accident

X Japan is widely credited as the originators of visual kei — the
elaborate, makeup-and-costume-heavy fusion of glam rock, punk, and
heavy/speed metal that became one of Japanese rock's most internationally
recognizable exports, directly shaping later acts like Dir en grey, Malice
Mizer, and Luna Sea. The band's own commercial breakthrough came with 1989's
"Blue Blood," a multi-platinum album built on three hit singles that
established them as a major national act, pairing genuinely virtuosic
musicianship with the theatrical visual presentation that would come to
define the whole genre after them.

### Tragedy, hiatus, and a genuinely improbable comeback

X Japan's history since its 1980s peak has been as dramatic as its stage
shows. Guitarist hide died in 1998 and bassist Taiji in 2011, and the band
worked through a lengthy hiatus and lineup changes before eventually
touring again as a reduced group. That comeback culminated in a genuinely
significant milestone: an October 2014 show at Madison Square Garden in New
York, a venue about as storied as American music has — a landmark for a
Japanese rock act, whatever the exact "first" claims attached to it turn
out to check out as.

## Where to start

"Blue Blood" (1989) is the clearest entry point — it's the album that
established the band nationally and still holds up as the fullest
statement of the visual-kei sound X Japan is credited with inventing. From
there, footage of the 2014 Madison Square Garden show (widely available on
video platforms) makes the case for the band as a live act, decades and
several lineup tragedies past their commercial peak.
