Sagamihara is, by Japanese city standards, a young place. It formed through
a series of 20th-century municipal mergers of smaller farming villages and
grew rapidly after WWII, first around a cluster of Japanese and then Allied
military facilities established in the area (Camp Zama, a major U.S. Army
installation, sits partly within the city), and later around manufacturing
plants that took advantage of its convenient position between Tokyo and
Kanagawa's coastal industrial belt. It only became Japan's 19th and most
recent designated city in 2010.

### Mission control for a piece of an asteroid

Sagamihara's most distinctive institution is the Japan Aerospace Exploration
Agency's (JAXA) Sagamihara Campus, the country's main center for space and
planetary science research since the 1960s. It served as mission control for
both Hayabusa (which returned the first-ever samples collected from an
asteroid's surface in 2010) and Hayabusa2 (which returned samples from
beneath an asteroid's surface in 2020) — missions that gave Japan's space
program international recognition well beyond its budget size relative to
NASA or ESA. The campus's public visitor center displays real mission
hardware and sample-research displays, drawing space enthusiasts from well
beyond the immediate area.

### A hillier, greener Tokyo suburb

Unlike the flatter, denser cities closer to Tokyo Bay, Sagamihara's northern
half rises into the foothills of Kanagawa's interior, giving it a
noticeably greener character around Lake Sagami and the nearby hills —
practical implications include better-known hiking and boating options than
most cities its size in the immediate Tokyo commuter belt offer.

## Getting there and getting around

Sagamihara is reachable from central Tokyo in roughly 40–60 minutes via JR
or Odakyu lines depending on the exact station, and functions primarily as
part of the greater Tokyo commuter belt rather than a typical standalone
tourist stop — most visitors come specifically for JAXA's facility or Lake
Sagami rather than as a general sightseeing destination.
