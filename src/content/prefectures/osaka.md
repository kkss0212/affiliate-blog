For most of the Edo period, Osaka — not Kyoto or Edo — was Japan's
commercial center: rice from across the country was shipped to Osaka's
warehouses to be traded and priced, effectively making it the country's
first futures market. That merchant identity outlasted the samurai era and
still shapes how Osaka thinks of itself: a working city, proud of being
built on trade rather than court politics.

### Osaka and the wider world

Osaka's clearest recent moment on the world stage was hosting Expo 2025 on
the artificial island of Yumeshima, its second World's Fair after the 1970
Osaka Expo — an event widely credited with introducing everyday Japanese
consumers to things like the videophone, the moving walkway, and canned
coffee. The 1970 Expo was also the first World's Fair held in Asia, timed
deliberately to showcase Japan's postwar recovery to a global audience,
much as Tokyo's 1964 Olympics had done six years earlier.

Further back, Osaka's port history is older and less triumphant: the city
was a key staging ground in the 16th-century unification wars, and Osaka
Castle — built by Toyotomi Hideyoshi in 1583 — was where his son made a
final, failed stand against the incoming Tokugawa shogunate in 1615, a
siege that effectively ended the Sengoku (Warring States) period.

### Neighbors: Osaka, Kyoto, and Kobe

Osaka, Kyoto, and Kobe together form the Keihanshin metropolitan area, all
reachable from each other in well under an hour by train. The
Osaka-Kyoto relationship carries the more famous rivalry — Osaka cast as
the loud, funny, merchant-minded counterpart to Kyoto's old-capital
refinement — while Kobe, the third corner, tends to sit slightly outside
that dynamic as the region's international port city.

## Getting there and getting around

Kansai International Airport (KIX), built on an artificial island in Osaka
Bay, is the main international gateway; Shin-Osaka Station connects to the
shinkansen network. The city itself runs on an extensive subway and JR
loop-line system similar in concept to Tokyo's Yamanote Line.
