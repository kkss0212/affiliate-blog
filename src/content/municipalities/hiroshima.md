The 1945 atomic bombing and its aftermath, covered on the Hiroshima
prefecture page, shaped one specific and unusual piece of Hiroshima City's
postwar legal foundation: the 1949 Hiroshima Peace Memorial City
Construction Law, a special act of Japan's national parliament — passed by
public referendum specific to the city — that formally designated
Hiroshima's reconstruction around the goal of world peace, granted the city
national funding support unavailable to most other rebuilding Japanese
cities, and set aside the land that became Peace Memorial Park. It remains
one of only two Japanese cities (alongside Nagasaki) with this kind of
dedicated national reconstruction law.

### A working city, not only a memorial

Rebuilt Hiroshima grew, over the following decades, into the Chugoku
region's largest city and a genuine industrial center in its own right —
most visibly through Mazda, founded here in 1920 (originally as a cork
manufacturer before pivoting to vehicles), whose headquarters and main
plant remain in the city's Fuchu area and whose distinctive rotary-engine
research became one of the more unusual technical bets in global automotive
history. A small number of Hiroshima's streetcars that survived the 1945
bombing continue running in ordinary daily passenger service today,
maintained not as museum pieces set apart from city life but as a quietly
continuous part of it — a detail many visitors don't learn until they're
already riding one.

### Everyday Hiroshima

Alongside its historical weight, Hiroshima has a distinct, well-defended
local food identity, most famously Hiroshima-style okonomiyaki — built in
layers of batter, cabbage, pork, and noodles rather than mixed together as
in the Osaka style — a rivalry locals take seriously enough that ordering
the "wrong" style in the wrong city can draw good-natured protest.

## Getting there and getting around

Hiroshima Station is a Sanyo Shinkansen stop, about 4 hours from Tokyo and
1 hour 20 minutes from Osaka; Hiroshima Airport connects to a handful of
domestic and international routes. The city's extensive streetcar network,
one of the most comprehensive in Japan, covers most major sights including
Peace Memorial Park, the castle, and the ferry terminal for Miyajima.
