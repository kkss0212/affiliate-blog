Perfume's three members — A-chan, Kashiyuka, and Nocchi — met as children
training at Actor's School Hiroshima, a local talent academy, and started
performing together as an idol group in the early 2000s, well before
"technopop trio" was a description anyone would have reached for. Their
sound changed direction after signing with producer Yasutaka Nakata, whose
heavily vocoder-processed, electronic production became inseparable from
Perfume's identity — a sharp turn away from the acoustic, girl-next-door
idol pop that dominated the genre at the time.

### From regional idol act to Coachella

Perfume's national breakthrough came with 2007's "Polyrhythm," picked up for
a widely seen Recruit television commercial, followed by a run of hits
("Chocolate Disco," "Love the World," "Baby Cruising Love") that established
them as one of Japan's defining pop acts of the late 2000s. What set them
apart internationally wasn't just the music, though — it was the staging.
Working with the technology and design studio Rhizomatiks, Perfume built
live shows around precisely synchronized real-time projection mapping,
motion-tracked visuals, and LED stage design years before that kind of
tech-driven concert production became a pop-industry standard. That
reputation carried them to Coachella and SXSW, festival stages few Japanese
pop acts had played before them, and built a durable following among
Western electronic-music and visual-arts audiences who came for the
technology as much as the songs.

### An unusually literal bridge to Silicon Valley

Perfume's most distinctive "wider world" moment wasn't a chart placement at
all: in 2013, they partnered directly with Google on the "Perfume Global
Site Project," an interactive site built on WebGL (a then-new browser
graphics standard) that let visitors control a real-time 3D dance
performance from their browser. It was as much a showcase for what the open
web could do as it was a Perfume release, and it became one of the most-
cited early examples of a mainstream pop act collaborating directly with
web-platform technology rather than just using it for marketing.

## Where to start

"Polyrhythm" (2007) is the obvious entry point — it's the single that
turned Perfume from a regional act into a national one, and it's the
clearest example of Nakata's vocoder-pop sound. From there, the 2008 album
*GAME* collects the run of singles that built their catalog into something
substantial, and live footage of their Rhizomatiks-era stage shows (widely
available on video platforms) makes the case for why they're worth
following as a visual act, not just a listening one.
