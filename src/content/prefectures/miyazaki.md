Miyazaki holds an unusual dual identity: in Japanese mythology, it's
where the story of the nation itself is said to begin, and in 20th-
century pop culture, it's where a generation of Japanese couples went to
pretend they were somewhere much further away.

### Miyazaki and the wider world

That second identity is the more surprising one. In the 1960s and '70s,
before overseas travel was affordable or straightforward for most
Japanese people, Miyazaki's mild, palm-fringed coastline was marketed
hard as a domestic substitute for an actual tropical honeymoon — hotels,
attractions, and entire coastal developments leaned into a deliberately
Polynesian-adjacent aesthetic, and the region became known nationwide,
half-affectionately, as "Japan's Hawaii." The branding faded once
international travel opened up and destinations like the real Hawaii
became reachable, but Miyazaki's surf culture and laid-back coastal
identity have outlasted the marketing campaign that created them.

The older story runs through Takachiho, in Miyazaki's mountainous
interior, where local shrines and the dramatic gorge itself are woven
into the Kojiki and Nihon Shoki — Japan's oldest chronicles — as the
place where the sun goddess Amaterasu's grandson descended to earth, and
where Japan's legendary first emperor, Jimmu, is said to have begun the
journey that founded the imperial line.

### Neighbors: Miyazaki and Kagoshima

Miyazaki shares its subtropical climate and much of its coastal
character with neighboring Kagoshima to the south, the two forming
Kyushu's warmest, most palm-and-volcano-inflected corner.

## Getting there and getting around

Miyazaki Airport has domestic flights and is a short bus or train ride
from central Miyazaki City; there's no shinkansen connection. Takachiho,
in the mountainous interior, is best reached by highway bus or rental
car.
