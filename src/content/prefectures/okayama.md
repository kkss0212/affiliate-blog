Okayama presents itself, cheerfully and consistently, as a sunny, gentle
place — a branding built on real, low-disaster-risk weather data, and
reinforced by the prefecture's adopted role as the hometown of Momotaro,
a folk hero literally born from a peach who is about as close to a
national mascot as Japanese folklore gets.

### Okayama and the wider world

Okayama's clearest connection to the West runs through a single
building. In 1930, the textile industrialist Ohara Magosaburo opened the
Ohara Museum of Art in Kurashiki — the first museum in Japan built
specifically to house Western art, at a time when almost no other
Japanese institution was collecting it seriously. The museum's founding
collection, assembled with the help of the Japanese painter Kojima
Torajiro, who traveled to Europe to acquire pieces directly, includes
genuine works by Monet, El Greco, Matisse, and Renoir — decades before
most Japanese audiences had any other opportunity to see original
Western paintings in person.

Okayama's other significant modern export is more practical: Kurashiki's
Kojima district is widely considered the birthplace of Japanese denim
manufacturing, and its selvedge denim — woven on old-style shuttle looms
— has become a genuinely prized, internationally recognized product among
denim enthusiasts worldwide, often commanding premium prices well above
mass-market jeans.

### Neighbors: Okayama and Hiroshima

Okayama and Hiroshima are Chugoku's two largest cities and share a mild,
friendly regional rivalry over which better represents the San'yo
(Seto Inland Sea-facing) side of the region — a smaller-scale echo of the
Osaka-Kyoto dynamic further east.

## Getting there and getting around

Okayama Station is a major Sanyo Shinkansen hub, about 3.5 hours from
Tokyo and 45 minutes from Hiroshima. Kurashiki is a short local train
ride from Okayama Station.
