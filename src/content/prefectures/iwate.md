For about a century, Iwate had something close to its own royal court.
Hiraizumi, now a quiet town, was the seat of the Northern Fujiwara clan —
a family that grew wealthy on Tohoku's gold deposits and horse trade and
built Chuson-ji, a temple complex whose Konjikido ("Golden Hall") is gilded
almost entirely in gold leaf, meant to rival the grandeur of Kyoto itself.
The Fujiwara were destroyed by Minamoto no Yoritomo in 1189, but Hiraizumi's
surviving buildings later became a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and are
believed to have influenced the gardens and temple design of Kyoto's own
Golden Pavilion two centuries later.

### Iwate and the wider world

Chuson-ji's gold wasn't just local wealth — some historians connect
Hiraizumi's Fujiwara-era prosperity to trade routes reaching as far as
continental Asia, part of a broader medieval Japanese gold trade that
reportedly reached Marco Polo, whose (heavily embellished) account of a
gold-roofed "Zipangu" is sometimes traced back to reports of exactly this
region.

Iwate's more recent and far heavier contact with the outside world came on
March 11, 2011, when the Tohoku earthquake and tsunami devastated its
coastline, killing thousands in towns like Rikuzentakata and Kamaishi. The
disaster drew an enormous international relief response and reconstruction
aid, and Iwate's coast — rebuilt with new sea walls, memorial parks, and a
"Miracle Pine" preserved as a survivor's memorial in Rikuzentakata — is
still, over a decade later, part of the region's story and identity.

### Neighbors: Iwate and the rest of Tohoku

Iwate shares the northern Tohoku coast with Aomori and Miyagi, all three
affected by the 2011 disaster to different degrees; inland, Morioka has
historically been a smaller, quieter counterpart to Sendai (Miyagi's
capital and Tohoku's largest city) rather than a rival to it.

## Getting there and getting around

Morioka Station is a Tohoku Shinkansen stop, roughly 2.5 hours from Tokyo;
Hanamaki Airport serves the prefecture's south. Iwate's size and low
population density make a rental car genuinely useful for reaching
Hiraizumi, Geibikei, and the coast.
