On August 6, 1945, the United States dropped the first nuclear weapon
used in warfare on Hiroshima. Widely cited estimates put the number of
deaths by the end of that year, from the blast itself and from
subsequent radiation effects, at roughly 140,000 people, in a city whose
prewar population was around 350,000. It remains, alongside the bombing
of Nagasaki three days later, the only use of nuclear weapons in an
armed conflict in history.

### Hiroshima and the wider world

What Hiroshima built afterward became, deliberately, an international
story in its own right. The Peace Memorial Park was constructed around
the hypocenter area starting in the 1950s, incorporating the skeletal
ruins of one of the only structures left standing nearby — now preserved
as the Atomic Bomb Dome and inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site
specifically as a monument against nuclear weapons. Hiroshima's mayors
have since become leading voices in international disarmament advocacy,
including through Mayors for Peace, an organization the city founded that
now includes thousands of member cities worldwide. In a notable, and
notably reconciliatory, gesture, Hiroshima maintains a sister-city
relationship with Honolulu, Hawaii — home to Pearl Harbor. That
international attention reached a visible peak in May 2023, when
Hiroshima hosted the G7 Summit, and the leaders of the United States,
United Kingdom, France, and other member nations visited the Peace
Memorial Park together, an event widely covered around the world.

### Neighbors: Hiroshima and Okayama

Hiroshima and Okayama are Chugoku's two largest cities, sharing the Seto
Inland Sea-facing San'yo side of the region and a mild, long-standing
rivalry over which city better represents it.

## Getting there and getting around

Hiroshima Station is a Sanyo Shinkansen stop, about 4 hours from Tokyo
and under an hour from Okayama. Miyajima is reached by a short JR ferry
from Miyajimaguchi Station, a 25-minute train ride from central
Hiroshima.
