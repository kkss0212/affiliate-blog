Kagoshima can make a reasonable claim to being where Japan's modern
relationship with the West actually starts. In 1543, a Chinese junk
carrying Portuguese traders was blown off course and landed on
Tanegashima, an island off Kagoshima's coast — the first documented
direct contact between Japan and Europeans. The Portuguese carried
matchlock firearms, which local lords quickly had reverse-engineered and
manufactured; within decades, guns had spread across Japan and
permanently changed how its wars were fought, arguably accelerating the
eventual unification of the country under a single government.

### Kagoshima and the wider world

That single accidental landing set off a chain of contact — traders,
then Jesuit missionaries (Francis Xavier himself landed nearby in
Kagoshima in 1549, beginning Japan's Christian century) — that would
eventually lead, centuries later, to exactly the kind of controlled,
narrow relationship Nagasaki's Dejima represented once the shogunate
decided open contact had gone too far. Kagoshima's Satsuma domain later
became one of the two powers, alongside Choshu (modern Yamaguchi), that
drove the shogunate's overthrow in 1868 — an alliance secured by Kochi's
Sakamoto Ryoma — making this one of the few threads on this whole site
that touches four separate prefectures directly.

Tanegashima's more recent chapter closes a genuinely remarkable loop: the
same island where Western firearms first reached Japanese shores in 1543
is now the site of the Tanegashima Space Center, from which Japan
launches its own rockets and satellites — arguably the most literal
before-and-after of "the West arrives with new technology" to "Japan
builds its own" anywhere in this entire guide.

### Neighbors: Kagoshima and Kumamoto

Satsuma's most famous son, Saigo Takamori, helped lead the movement that
overthrew the shogunate — then, a decade later, led the 1877 Satsuma
Rebellion against the government he'd helped install, besieging
Kumamoto Castle before dying at the Battle of Shiroyama in Kagoshima
City itself, bringing the story that started with Ryoma's alliance to
its final, tragic close.

## Getting there and getting around

Kagoshima Chuo Station is the southern terminus of the Kyushu
Shinkansen, about 4 hours from Hakata (Fukuoka). Sakurajima is a
15-minute ferry ride from central Kagoshima; Tanegashima is reached by
plane or ferry from Kagoshima port.
