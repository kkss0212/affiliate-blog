For much of the Edo period, Osaka — then written Ōzaka — functioned as
Japan's economic engine even though political power sat in Edo (Tokyo) and
the emperor's court remained in Kyoto. Daimyo across the country shipped
their domains' rice surplus to Osaka to be sold and converted into cash,
and the city grew into a dense network of merchant houses, warehouses, and
canals that earned it the nickname "tenka no daidokoro" — the nation's
kitchen.

### An early futures market, four centuries early

Osaka's most significant, and least visible, contribution to global economic
history happened at the Dojima Rice Exchange, established in the 1730s to
formalize what had already become an active trade in rice-delivery
contracts. Merchants there developed standardized contracts for rice to be
delivered at a future date and price — a structure widely credited by
economic historians as one of the world's earliest organized futures
markets, predating Chicago's grain futures exchanges by more than a century.
That commercial, deal-making culture is often cited as the root of Osaka's
enduring reputation, within Japan, as blunter, funnier, and more openly
money-minded than the more reserved Tokyo or tradition-bound Kyoto.

### Osaka today: comedy, food, and a second Expo

That merchant-city energy still shows up clearly in Osaka's modern culture.
The city is the historic home of manzai comedy and the base of Yoshimoto
Kogyo, Japan's dominant comedy talent agency, which supplies a large
share of the comedians appearing on national television. Osaka's food
culture — takoyaki, okonomiyaki, kushikatsu — carries a similar reputation
for being unpretentious and abundant rather than refined. In 2025, Osaka
returned to the world stage as a host city for a World Expo for the first
time since 1970, building Expo 2025 on the newly created artificial island
of Yumeshima in Osaka Bay.

## Getting there and getting around

Shin-Osaka Station is a major Tokaido/Sanyo Shinkansen stop, about 2 hours
30 minutes from Tokyo and 15 minutes from Kyoto; Kansai International
Airport, on its own artificial island, is Osaka's main international
gateway. The city has an extensive subway and JR loop-line network covering
all major districts.
