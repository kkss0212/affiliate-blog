No prefecture on this site has a longer or more direct relationship with
the West than Nagasaki — for roughly two and a half centuries, it wasn't
just connected to it, it was Japan's only legal point of contact with it
at all.

### Nagasaki and the wider world

When the Tokugawa shogunate closed Japan to almost all foreign contact
starting in the 1630s, it made one narrow, deliberate exception: Dutch
traders, confined to Dejima, a small fan-shaped artificial island built
in Nagasaki harbor specifically to contain and monitor them. From 1641
until Japan's reopening in 1854, Dejima was the single channel through
which Western goods, medical knowledge, and science reached Japan at
all — a field of study Japanese scholars called Rangaku ("Dutch
Learning"), covering everything from anatomy texts to astronomy. That
narrow but continuous channel meant Japan was never as totally cut off
from Western scientific developments as its closed borders suggested,
and helped the country adapt unusually fast once it reopened for real in
the mid-1800s.

Nagasaki's other deep connection to the West predates even Dejima:
Portuguese missionaries built one of Japan's largest early Christian
communities here starting in the 1570s, before Christianity was banned
in 1614 and its practitioners violently suppressed. A community of
"Hidden Christians" kept their faith alive in secret for well over two
centuries, passing down prayers and practices without any priest or
church, until a group revealed themselves to a French priest at the
newly built Oura Church in 1865 — an event the Vatican later described
as a "miracle of the Orient," and the sites connected to this history are
now collectively a UNESCO World Heritage listing.

Nagasaki's more recent, far heavier connection to the world came on
August 9, 1945, when it became the target of the second nuclear weapon
used in warfare, three days after Hiroshima. Widely cited estimates put
deaths by the end of that year at roughly 70,000. Like Hiroshima,
Nagasaki has since built an active, sustained identity around peace
advocacy and nuclear disarmament, maintained through its own Peace Park,
annual memorial ceremonies, and international outreach.

### Neighbors: Nagasaki and Saga

Nagasaki's neighbor Saga helped guard and administer this singular
trading arrangement under the shogunate's system, making the two
prefectures' histories genuinely intertwined well before either was
called by its modern name.

## Getting there and getting around

Nagasaki Station is reached from Hakata (Fukuoka) via the Nishi-Kyushu
Shinkansen (with a transfer at Takeo-Onsen) in about 1.5-2 hours total.
Central Nagasaki's main sites, including Dejima and Glover Garden, are
walkable or reachable by the city's tram network.
